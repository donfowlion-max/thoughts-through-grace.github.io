When Parents Forsake, God Takes Us Up!
The bond between parents and children is one of the deepest connections in human life. Parents are meant to be the first safe place, the first reflection of unconditional love. Yet, for many, this bond is fractured. Abandonment, neglect, and rejection leave wounds that cut to the core of identity.
This pain is not limited to childhood. Some carry it into adulthood, even within marriage. To live under the same roof yet feel unseen and unloved is a silent form of marginalization. Social anxiety can deepen this isolation, making connection feel impossible.
Into this reality, Scripture speaks hope: “When my father and my mother forsake me, then the Lord will take me up” (Psalm 27:10). God Himself steps into the gap left by human failure. Where earthly love falters, divine love sustains. The marginalized — whether abandoned by parents, overlooked by society, or neglected within marriage — are not forgotten. They are lifted, embraced, and given belonging in God’s household.
Reflection Questions Have you ever felt unseen by those closest to you?
How does Psalm 27:10 reshape your understanding of rejection?
In what ways can you invite God into spaces where you feel alone?
